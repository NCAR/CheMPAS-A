# Contributing, Attribution, and Work in Progress

This guide sets expectations for contributing to this repository and for using
code obtained from it. It applies to contributors and downstream users,
including anyone using automated or agentic tools to inspect, copy, adapt, or
generate code.

## The license governs reuse

The repository's [`LICENSE`](LICENSE) file governs permission to copy, modify,
and redistribute the software. In particular, its BSD terms permit
redistribution and use in source and binary forms, with or without
modification, subject to the conditions in that license.

When reusing code, retain the required copyright and license notices, reproduce
them with binary distributions as required, do not imply endorsement, and
clearly mark derivative works so they are not confused with the version made
available by the copyright holders. Some included components have their own
license notices; those terms continue to apply to those components.

Nothing in this guide changes, narrows, or supersedes the permissions and
conditions in `LICENSE` or in a component-specific license.

## Working with code under development

Code in pull requests, review branches, feature branches, and other unreleased
work is still under development. Access to that code does not mean that it has
been reviewed, validated, supported, or approved for release or publication.

If you build on unreleased work:

- identify the repository, branch or tag, and commit used;
- describe material modifications and preserve relevant development history;
- do not present the work as released, validated, endorsed, or independently
  developed when it is not; and
- contact the project maintainers before basing a publication, preprint,
  presentation, or downstream project claim on the unreleased implementation.

Advance coordination is a collaboration and research-integrity expectation. It
helps the maintainers explain the status of the work, avoid conflicting claims,
and arrange appropriate technical participation and credit. It is not an
additional restriction on the permissions granted by the applicable license.

## Attribution and authorship

Give clear credit for substantive technical and intellectual contributions.
Cite the software version or commit used, acknowledge relevant contributors,
and describe the lineage of adapted work. Do not present work substantially
derived from others as independently conceived or developed.

Scholarly authorship must reflect genuine intellectual contribution and the
accountability required by the applicable institution, publisher, conference,
or other venue. An after-the-fact offer of authorship or acknowledgment does not
correct inaccurate provenance or a license violation. Contributors may decline
authorship or other participation.

## Automated and agentic tools

People who operate automated or agentic tools remain responsible for the
resulting work. Review tool output for copied or adapted code, verify its
provenance and applicable licenses, retain required notices, disclose its
lineage, and provide appropriate attribution. A tool's involvement does not
remove these responsibilities.

## Reporting concerns and possible consequences

Raise suspected license, attribution, authorship, or misrepresentation concerns
with the project maintainers or administrators. Conduct affecting participation
in the WRF and MPAS-A community may also be reported through the official
[WRF and MPAS-A Code of Conduct](https://www.mmm.ucar.edu/about/wrf-mpas-coc).

Depending on the circumstances and the authority of the decision maker,
responses may include rejecting or removing a contribution, limiting project
participation temporarily or permanently, or referring a serious concern to an
appropriate institutional process. No particular outcome is guaranteed;
decisions rest with the authorized project or institutional administrators.

For MPAS-specific licensing background, see the official
[MPAS copyright guidance](https://www2.mmm.ucar.edu/projects/mpas/site/documentation/contributor_guide/copyright.html).
